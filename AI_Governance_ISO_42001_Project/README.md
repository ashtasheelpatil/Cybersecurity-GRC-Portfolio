# AI Governance & ISO/IEC 42001 Implementation Project

## Purpose
A practical, portfolio-ready Artificial Intelligence Management System (AIMS) project aligned to ISO/IEC 42001 concepts. It demonstrates how an organization can establish governance, risk management, impact assessment, lifecycle controls, supplier oversight, auditing, and continual improvement for AI systems.

## Project Scenario
**Organization:** ApexAI Solutions Pvt. Ltd.  
**Industry:** Technology / SaaS  
**Scope:** AI systems developed, procured, deployed, or operated by the organization, including AI-enabled SaaS products and internal AI use cases.

## Project Objectives
1. Establish an AI governance structure.
2. Define an AI management system and its scope.
3. Identify AI-related risks and impacts.
4. Maintain an AI system inventory.
5. Implement governance and operational controls.
6. Assess AI impacts across affected stakeholders.
7. Manage data, model, supplier, and lifecycle risks.
8. Prepare for internal audit and management review.
9. Track incidents, nonconformities, corrective actions, and improvement.

## Folder Structure
- `01_Project_Governance` — charter, roles, project plan
- `02_Organization_Context` — context, interested parties, scope
- `03_AI_Governance_Policy` — AI policy, principles, governance model
- `04_AI_Risk_Management` — AI risk methodology and risk register
- `05_AI_Impact_Assessment` — impact assessment methodology and register
- `06_AI_System_Inventory` — AI system register and lifecycle classification
- `07_ISO_42001_Control_Implementation` — control applicability and evidence
- `08_Data_and_AI_Lifecycle` — data governance, development, validation, monitoring
- `09_Third_Party_AI_Risk` — vendor and foundation-model risk
- `10_Incident_and_Nonconformity` — incidents, complaints, nonconformities, CAPA
- `11_Internal_Audit_and_Management_Review` — audit and management review
- `12_Continual_Improvement` — improvement register and lessons learned

## Important Note
This is a practical implementation/portfolio project, not a copy of the ISO standard. ISO/IEC 42001 requirements should be checked against the officially licensed standard and the organization's actual context before certification or formal compliance claims.
